# Estimated Muscle Soreness

The Profile body map estimates current soreness on a common 0–100 scale for each muscle and muscle group. Tap a group to inspect individual muscles and the workouts contributing to its estimate.

- Checked, completed sets count, including bodyweight exercises with zero added weight and assisted bodyweight exercises.
- More work and harder effort generally increase the estimate. Optional effort and warm-up controls help distinguish challenging sets from easy practice. Enter assistance as a positive amount; the screen supplies the minus sign.
- For holds, enter seconds. Effort describes how close you were to stopping.
- Soreness can rise after training, usually peaking in the following days before fading. A higher forecast over the next 48 hours is shown when relevant. Repeated familiar exercise produces a more modest estimated response.
- Missing information uses labeled assumptions. Older workouts still contribute without invented historical body mass or effort. Gray means unavailable/unmapped, not recovered.
- Group scores combine the weighted average with the highest substantially involved muscle, so a sore individual muscle remains visible. Select the group to see exactly which muscles contribute.

Compare groups when deciding what to train, and compare the estimate with how you actually feel. A score is an estimate, not a measured pain percentage or a guarantee that a muscle is ready. The exact scale and coefficients have not been validated against individual soreness reports.
